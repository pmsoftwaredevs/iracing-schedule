Quick guide, if any of this is new

What's a Championship?
iRacing runs most series as a Championship: the same car-and-track combination repeats every week of the 13-week season, with points toward a season standing. Tick the ones you race above and this planner builds your calendar around them.
What's a Special Event?
A one-off race outside the regular championships — endurance classics, all-star showdowns, and the like — held on its own fixed date rather than every week.
What do the R / D / C / B / A / P badges mean?
iRacing's license ladder, from Rookie up through Class D, C, B, and A, to Pro/World Champion. A championship's badge shows the minimum license it requires; use the License filter above to narrow the list to what you can actually join.
What are the small icons next to each championship?
The racing discipline — oval, sports car, formula car, dirt oval, or dirt road. The Category filter above uses the same icons to let you show only the ones you race.
Do I need an iRacing account to use this planner?
No — this site only reads iRacing's public schedule, and nothing you pick here is sent anywhere or tied to an account. You will need an iRacing account (and a subscription) to actually enter a race — see iRacing.com.
How does the timing work?
Most championships run several sessions a week. Add a slot for each day/time you actually race, and the planner converts it to your local timezone automatically.
How do I get this into my calendar app?
Copy the subscribe URL from the green box above and add it as a subscription (not a one-time import) in Google Calendar, Apple Calendar, or Outlook — the “?” button next to it has exact steps for each. It stays in sync automatically if you come back and change your picks, so you only set it up once.

We'd like to save your calendar selections, timezone, and license/category filters in a cookie on this device, so they're waiting for you next time you visit. Optional, and nothing is tracked or shared.
